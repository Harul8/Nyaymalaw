## Assessment of the original 44 requirements

They provide a strong foundation: adaptive briefing, professional conversation,
attribution, retrieval, adverse material, independent assessment, calibrated
advice, inspectable sources, corrections, permissions and evaluation. The main
deficit is **depth of judgment**, not a missing high-level phase. For example,
“analyse issues” does not adequately tell a builder how to distinguish a fatal
missing premise from several favourable but non-dispositive points.

No finite list proves that every legal problem is covered. Nor does an advocacy
manual prove that an AI can exercise professional judgment. These additions make
the intended behaviour more testable; they do not certify senior-counsel quality.

## Research method and transfer limits

Primary material was selected from Indian courts/judicial institutions and
professional advocacy bodies, plus a research-backed report on witness memory.
This combines an Indian judge's first-hand account of distinguished advocacy,
judicial teaching, professional competence standards and a bounded empirical
finding. These are different kinds of evidence, not interchangeable endorsements.

Foreign guidance informs transferable practice only. It supplies **no Indian
procedural deadline, admissibility rule, disclosure duty or professional licence**.
Each such proposition must still be established for the actual forum, date and
task from current applicable sources. A minimum competence framework is not an
empirical description of every leading advocate. Anecdotes illustrate a principle;
they are not success-rate evidence or runtime examples for NM to imitate.

The workbook's engineering contracts, interface behaviour, evidence states and
acceptance checks are original NM design inferences. The sources below support
the underlying professional principles, not a ready-made software architecture.

## Primary sources and what they contribute

### 1. Indian judicial account: focus and professional trust

[Supreme Court Chronicle, January 2025](https://cdnbbsr.s3waas.gov.in/s3ec0490f1f4972d133619a60c30f3559e/uploads/2025/01/2025011015.pdf),
interview with Justice C.T. Ravikumar, printed pp9–12, especially p12.
He recalls Fali Nariman isolating a decisive issue in a complex matter and
describes the importance of candour and court assistance. This supports focused
issue selection and professional trust, not a rule that every matter has one
winning point. Used for LB-47 and the rationale for LB-51.

### 2. Indian professional independence

[R. Muthukrishnan v Registrar General, Supreme Court judgment, 28 January 2019](https://api.sci.gov.in/supremecourt/2016/23266/23266_2016_Judgement_28-Jan-2019.pdf),
PDF pp59–60: discussion of independent judgment, restrained professional conduct
and the advocate's responsibilities beyond mechanically carrying out client
wishes. Used for LB-17/23/51. This is not a fresh consolidated audit of the BCI
rules. NM must not translate candour into automatic disclosure, withdrawal or
reporting; confidentiality, current duties and professional decisions still matter.

### 3. Judicial teaching: fit the argument to the decision

[Justice Rachel Pepper, A Basic Guide to Good Written and Oral Advocacy, 2015](https://designsystem.dcj.nsw.gov.au/content/dam/dcj/ctsd/lec/documents/speeches-and-papers/PepperJ%20A%20basic%20guide%20to%20written%20and%20oral%20advocacy%20ANU%20160315.pdf),
paras 3–7, 11, 13–21 and 37–43. The court-hosted guide discusses relief and
power, coherent propositions, relevant authority, mastery of the record and
responsive answers. Used for LB-45/46/47/51/54. It is Australian advocacy teaching,
not Indian law. Its context-specific tactics and procedural assumptions have not
been adopted wholesale.

### 4. Indian judicial training: preparation and procedural readiness

[Justice Talwant Singh, Court & Case Management, NJA programme material 2019–20](https://nja.gov.in/Concluded_Programmes/2019-20/P-1167_PPTs/4.COURT%20&%20CASE%20MANAGEMENT.pdf),
PDF pp9–10 and 13. The material connects procedural preparation and concise
written submissions with identifying real issues. Used as supporting context for
LB-46/47. Older comparative case-management provisions and statistics in the
presentation are not adopted as current Indian rules.

### 5. Research-backed caution: honest memory can be distorted

[ICC Commission report on the Accuracy of Fact Witness Memory in International Arbitration, 2020](https://iccwbo.org/wp-content/uploads/sites/3/2020/11/icc-arbitration-adr-commission-report-on-accuracy-fact-witness-memory-international-arbitration-english-version.pdf),
paras 2.29–2.31, 3.1 and 5.10. The report combines research review with a
commissioned experiment and discusses question-induced distortion and the source
of recollection. Used for LB-05/15/16/49. NM's safeguards preserve uncertainty and
source attribution; they do not presume any particular witness truthful or
untruthful. This research does not validate an AI credibility detector or justify
direct witness contact. No report template or interview script is reproduced.

### 6. Indian mediation practice: objectives and workable resolution

[Mediation Training Manual of India, official GSLSA/NALSA publication page](https://gujarat.nalsa.gov.in/en/publication/mediation-training-manual-of-india-english/),
[manual PDF](https://cdnbbsr.s3waas.gov.in/s3f8df2e15374e3dc37766e59ac494f0fd/uploads/2025/04/202504192096569083.pdf),
chapters VI, X and XIII; printed pp30–33, 53–60 and 69–70. It addresses interests,
reality testing, alternatives and the lawyer's role around settlement. Used for
LB-03/22/52. The website's 2025 upload is not proof of the manual's legal currency.
No mandatory mediation path, discouragement of legitimate reconsideration, or
assumption of mediator neutrality is imported into NM's advocate-support role.

### 7. Professional competence: analysis, limits and reflection

[Bar Standards Board Professional Statement](https://www.barstandardsboard.org.uk/asset/0279B209-DAB6-40C9-A554AF54994E2566/),
sections 1.5–1.7, 1.9–1.15 and 2.5. The framework describes analysis, independent
advice, research beyond law, competent drafting and reflective correction. Used
for LB-48/53/55/56 and refinements to LB-39/40. It is an England-and-Wales
competence baseline, not Indian regulation or proof of elite performance. NM's
proof-plan, instrument-dependency and controlled-learning mechanisms are design
inferences from those broad competencies.

### 8. Advocacy teaching: respond, rather than merely recite

[Inns of Court College of Advocacy, What is Advocacy?](https://www.icca.ac.uk/post-qualification-training/cpd/advocacy-training/what-is-advocacy/).
The professional body's account emphasises issue focus, perspective, listening
and concise presentation. Used to corroborate LB-04/47/54. It does not establish
that a model can predict a judge or replace professional representation.

### Access limitations

The Supreme Court AOR lecture listing and a BCI rules PDF endpoint did not yield
usable full documents in this session. Their contents are not relied upon.
No private interviews with senior advocates or empirical evaluation of NM were
performed. The source survey therefore informs a stronger plan, not an exhaustive
statement of Indian advocacy practice or a current professional-rules certificate.

These are **capabilities available when relevant**, not twelve additional intake
screens, compulsory questions, a larger monolithic prompt or twelve mandated
agents. The advocate remains the professional user and authorised decision-maker.
The important visible product is a concise explanation whose factual, evidential,
legal and inferential dependencies can be inspected and challenged—not a display
of hidden model deliberation.

## What still prevents a claim of completeness

Before building an affected slice, LB-43 must reconcile these requirements and
clauses to real delivery/contract/evidence owners. A green existing backlog check
does not yet certify this workbook-only scope. LB-44 still requires supported
practice areas, stages and source coverage; applicable processing permissions;
professional reviewer/rubric/population; quality thresholds; cost/latency budgets;
and accountable release decisions.

Then validate the selected scope at four distinct levels: preserved data and
enforced permissions; correct source/inference relationships; real user-led
browser/API operation; and held-out professional assessment of substantive
judgment. Use failure-planting and irrelevant-preference changes as well as
material-evidence changes. Report what was not assessed. Do not accept outcome,
eloquence, citations, user agreement or a second model's approval as sole proof.

This pass changes the **plan**, not the application. Media, real transcription,
live-model evaluations, professional sign-off and other previously parked
acceptance work remain open.
