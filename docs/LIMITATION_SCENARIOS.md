# Limitation scenarios: draft for the owner's confirmation

**Status:** draft, 15 October 2026. Item 9 (SEQ.7), slice A4. These scenarios become the passing standard for the limitation calculation only after the owner, or an advocate the owner names, confirms or corrects each one.

**How to review:** for each scenario, reply *confirm*, or give the correction and its reason. The counting rules (section 1) matter as much as the dates: the code will apply them to every case.

**Basis:** every period is quoted from the held text of the Limitation Act, 1963 or the Negotiable Instruments Act, 1881 (checked 15 October 2026). All dates and weekdays were computed by code. "Today" is Thursday, 15 October 2026.

---

## 1. Counting rules the code will apply

| # | Rule | Held basis | Please confirm |
|---|------|-----------|----------------|
| R1 | Day periods: the starting day is left out, so the last day is the starting date plus the number of days. | Limitation Act s.12(1): "the day from which such period is to be reckoned, shall be excluded." | |
| R2 | Month and year periods are calendar months and years. With the starting day left out, the period ends on the same date of the later month or year (Saketh India: three months from 5 February ended on 5 May). | s.12(1); *Saketh India v India Securities* (1999), which also says "A month is to be reckoned according to the British Calendar". | |
| R3 | **Where that date does not exist** (29 February + 3 years), the period ends on the last day of that month (28 February). | No held text found that settles it. | **Yes: this needs your ruling** (see scenario 6). |
| R4 | Copy time (appeals): the days from applying for the certified copy to the day it was ready are left out, **counting both days**. Time the court took to prepare the decree before the copy was applied for is not left out. | s.12(2), (3) and the Explanation. | **Yes: the "both days" convention** (scenario 7). |
| R5 | A written acknowledgment signed before expiry, or a part payment made before expiry and acknowledged in the payer's writing, starts a fresh period from the date it was signed or paid. | ss.18 and 19 (with the proviso to s.19). | |
| R6 | Delay condonation is never computed: s.5 (appeals and applications, **not suits**, and **not Order XXI applications**) and the proviso to NI Act s.142 are the court's discretion. The answer says whether condonation is even available. | s.5; NI Act s.142(1)(b) proviso. | |
| R7 | Court closure (s.4) is flagged, never applied: when the last day may be a day the court is closed, the answer says the filing may be made "on the day when the court reopens", and does not guess that day without the court calendar. | s.4 and its Explanation. | |
| R8 | Special laws: the NI Act's own periods govern its timeline. A cheque-bounce complaint's month is counted with the first day left out (Saketh India). | Limitation Act s.29(2); NI Act ss.138, 142; *Saketh India*. | |
| R9 | Only Acts listed as current are calculated: the Limitation Act, the NI Act and the CPC. For any other Act, the provision is shown and the calculation is reported **not assessed (currency not verified)**. | Owner decision, 15 Oct 2026. | |
| R10 | Dates are as reported. The answer names the source of each date (the advocate's message, a document page, a recording), and marks dates the AI read from a scan or recording for checking against the original. | Owner decisions, items 6 and 8. | |

## 2. Scenarios

### Scenario 1: money lent, no acknowledgment
**Advocate says:** "My client lent ₹3,00,000 to the defendant on 10 March 2023. Nothing has been repaid. Can we file a recovery suit now?"
**Provision:** Article 19, "For money payable for money lent. | Three years | When the loan is made."
**Working:** loan made 10 Mar 2023 → leave out that day (R1/R2) → three years end **10 Mar 2026 (Tuesday)**.
**Expected:** a suit filed today is **out of time**. s.5 cannot rescue a *suit*. The answer must ask whether the defendant signed any written acknowledgment, or made any part payment acknowledged in writing, before 10 Mar 2026 (ss.18, 19). If the loan was payable on demand, Article 21 gives the same start ("When the loan is made"), so the same date.

### Scenario 2: acknowledgment in writing before expiry
**Advocate says:** as scenario 1, plus "On 1 February 2025 the defendant signed a letter admitting he owes the money."
**Provisions:** Article 19; s.18(1), "a fresh period of limitation shall be computed from the time when the acknowledgment was so signed."
**Working:** the acknowledgment was signed 1 Feb 2025, before the original expiry (10 Mar 2026) → fresh three years from 1 Feb 2025 → **1 Feb 2028 (Tuesday)**.
**Expected:** **in time**. The answer must keep the conditions visible: signed by the defendant (or someone through whom he derives liability), made before expiry, and an admission of the liability. Whether a particular message (for example WhatsApp) is a "signed" writing is left to the advocate; code does not decide it.

### Scenario 3: part payment before expiry
**Advocate says:** as scenario 1, plus "On 5 January 2026 the defendant paid ₹50,000 by bank transfer."
**Provisions:** s.19, "a fresh period of limitation shall be computed from the time when the payment was made", with the proviso that "an acknowledgment of the payment appears in the handwriting of, or in a writing signed by, the person making the payment."
**Working:** the payment (5 Jan 2026) was before 10 Mar 2026. If the proviso is met, the fresh period ends **5 Jan 2029 (Friday)**; if not, the original **10 Mar 2026** stands.
**Expected:** **both results shown**, with the question that decides between them: is there a writing in the payer's hand or signed by the payer acknowledging the payment? A bank record alone is not assumed to meet the proviso.

### Scenario 4: promissory note payable on demand
**Advocate says:** "We hold a promissory note dated 1 August 2023, payable on demand, for ₹5,00,000. Nothing paid."
**Provision:** Article 35, "On a bill of exchange or promissory note payable on demand and not accompanied by any writing restraining or postponing the right to sue. | Three years | The date of the bill or note."
**Working:** note dated 1 Aug 2023 → **1 Aug 2026 (Saturday)**. s.4 is flagged: if the court was closed that day, the suit could have been filed when it reopened.
**Expected:** **out of time today** in any case, unless ss.18 or 19 apply. The answer asks about acknowledgments, payments, and any writing postponing the right to sue (which would take the note outside Article 35).

### Scenario 5: specific performance
**Advocate says:** (a) "The agreement of sale fixed 30 June 2024 for completing the sale; the seller did not complete." (b) Variant: "No date was fixed; the seller's letter refusing to perform reached my client on 12 February 2024."
**Provision:** Article 54, "For specific performance of a contract. | Three years | The date fixed for the performance, or, if no such date is fixed, when the plaintiff has notice that performance is refused."
**Working:** (a) **30 Jun 2027 (Wednesday)**; (b) **12 Feb 2027 (Friday)**.
**Expected:** both **in time**. For (b) the answer asks whether the client had notice of refusal any earlier (for example orally or by conduct), because an earlier notice would start time earlier.

### Scenario 6: breach of contract on a leap day *(needs your ruling)*
**Advocate says:** "The supplier was bound to deliver by 29 February 2024 and did not."
**Provision:** Article 55, "When the contract is broken …", three years.
**Working:** three years from 29 Feb 2024; there is no 29 Feb 2027. Under R3 the last day is **28 Feb 2027 (Sunday)**; the other reading gives 1 Mar 2027 (Monday).
**Expected (draft):** state **28 Feb 2027** as the safe last day, mention the other reading, and flag s.4 (a Sunday). **Please rule on R3.**

### Scenario 7: first appeal to the High Court, with copy time *(confirm the copy-day convention)*
**Advocate says:** "The district court's decree is dated 2 June 2026. We applied for the certified copy on 5 June; it was ready on 20 June. When must the appeal to the High Court be filed?"
**Provisions:** Article 116(a), "to a High Court from any decree or order. | Ninety days | The date of the decree or order."; s.12(2).
**Working:** 90 days from 2 Jun 2026, that day left out → 31 Aug 2026; copy time 5 to 20 June, both days counted = 16 days → **16 Sep 2026 (Wednesday)**.
**Expected:** a filing today is **late**. An application under s.5 is available (an appeal); the court decides it. **Please confirm R4.** If the application day is not left out, the date is 15 Sep 2026.

### Scenario 8: appeal to the district court, copy dates unknown
**Advocate says:** "The junior civil judge's decree is dated 31 January 2026. Can we still appeal?"
**Provision:** Article 116(b), "to any other court from any decree or order. | Thirty days | The date of the decree or order."
**Working:** 30 days from 31 Jan 2026 → **2 Mar 2026 (Monday)**, before any copy time.
**Expected:** the answer **asks when the certified copy was applied for and ready** before calling it late, because s.12(2) may extend the date. It also says that, if late, an application under s.5 is possible.

### Scenario 9: setting aside an ex parte decree
**Advocate says:** (a) "An ex parte decree was passed against my client on 10 July 2026; he had been served." (b) Variant: "He was never properly served (only substituted service under Order V rule 20); he first learnt of the decree on 1 October 2026."
**Provision:** Article 123, "Thirty days | The date of the decree or where the summons or notice was not duly served, when the applicant had knowledge of the decree", with its Explanation that substituted service under Order V rule 20 "shall not be deemed to be due service".
**Working:** (a) **9 Aug 2026 (Sunday)**, with s.4 flagged; (b) **31 Oct 2026 (Saturday)**.
**Expected:** (a) **late**, with s.5 available (an application); (b) **in time**. The answer asks how service was actually made, since that decides which starting point applies.

### Scenario 10: execution of a money decree
**Advocate says:** "We have a money decree that became enforceable on 15 November 2014. Nothing has been recovered."
**Provision:** Article 136, "Twelve years | When the decree or order becomes enforceable …"
**Working:** **15 Nov 2026 (Sunday)**, with s.4 flagged.
**Expected:** **in time, with 31 days left**, so urgent. The answer must say that s.5 does **not** apply to Order XXI applications ("other than an application under any of the provisions of Order XXI"), so there is no condonation if this date is missed. It asks whether the decree directs payment by instalments or on dates (which changes the start).

### Scenario 11: the cheque-bounce timeline
**Advocate says:** "Cheque dated 1 August 2026 for ₹2,00,000, presented 10 August, bank's return memo received by my client on 18 August (funds insufficient). Notice sent 25 August, delivered to the drawer on 29 August. No payment."
**Provisions:** NI Act s.138 proviso (a) "within a period of six months from the date on which it is drawn or within the period of its validity, whichever is earlier", (b) notice "within thirty days of the receipt of information by him from the bank", (c) payment "within fifteen days of the receipt of the said notice"; s.142(1)(b) "within one month of the date on which the cause of action arises".
**Working:**
1. Presentation: six months from 1 Aug 2026 runs to 1 Feb 2027; presented 10 Aug. The cheque's **validity period is not in held law**, so the answer asks for it or flags it.
2. Notice: thirty days from 18 Aug → by **17 Sep 2026 (Thursday)**; given 25 Aug → in time.
3. Payment: fifteen days from receipt on 29 Aug → last day to pay **13 Sep 2026 (Sunday)**; cause of action arises **14 Sep 2026 (Monday)**.
4. Complaint: one month from 14 Sep, first day left out (Saketh India) → **14 Oct 2026 (Wednesday)**.

**Expected:**
- A complaint filed **12 Sep 2026** is **premature**: *Yogendra Pratap Singh v Savitri Pandey* (2014) holds it does not disclose the cause of action, and a fresh complaint is needed.
- One filed **14 Oct 2026** is **in time**.
- One filed **today (15 Oct)** is **one day late**: cognizance is possible only if the court accepts sufficient cause (s.142 proviso, flagged and not computed).

**Please confirm** that the thirty-day notice limit is met by the date the notice is *given* (sent), not received.

### Scenario 12: the last day falls on a Sunday
**Advocate says:** "Decree of the junior civil judge dated 4 September 2026; we have not appealed yet."
**Provision:** Article 116(b), thirty days.
**Working:** **4 Oct 2026 (Sunday)**.
**Expected:** the answer states 4 Oct 2026, then: "if the court was closed on that day, the appeal could be filed on the day it reopened (s.4)", without naming that day. It asks for the copy dates (s.12(2)). Without them, a filing today is late, and an application under s.5 is available.

### Scenario 13: an Act whose currency is not verified
**Advocate says:** "My client bought a defective car on 1 March 2024. Until when can he file a consumer complaint?"
**Expected:** **not assessed**. The held Consumer Protection Act is the 1986 Act, which has been replaced. Under R9 the answer gives **no deadline**, says why, and suggests the advocate check the current Act. It must never compute a period from the held 1986 text.

## 3. Points that need your ruling

1. **R3** (scenario 6): when the matching date does not exist, does the period end on the last day of that month (28 Feb), or the next day (1 Mar)?
2. **R4** (scenario 7): copy time counted from the application day to the ready day, both days included?
3. **Scenario 11**: notice limit met by the date the notice is sent?
4. **Anything missing** from the population: for example s.6 (legal disability), s.14 (time in a court without jurisdiction), s.17 (fraud or mistake), or appeals to the Supreme Court. I suggest these are flagged as "may extend; not computed" in the first build.
