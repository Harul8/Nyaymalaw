Description:
An answer covering one or more disputes opens with one 'In short' instead of a greeting, the overview's paragraphs, the disputes' summaries and a separate table of time limits. It holds a row for each dispute, most urgent first, with three columns: what to do; by when; what to have ready (what the case most needs that is not yet in hand, from the dispute's checklist). Under the rows, one 'Do first' line: what to do first and why. By when comes from code's own time limits, as a date with its weekday and the days left; a limit not worked out says so in a few plain words and why (P36), never a date code did not give. The overview, still written last from the sections shown (P26), writes the Do-first line and, only where it matters, what ties the disputes together or what none of them could establish, each once. A correction that changes the advice is still called out at the top (P27). Each row appears as its dispute's section is shown; the Do-first line when the overview lands. The full Working of a time limit stays a click away. A dispute held back, or shown only as code's note (P25), says so in its row in one plain line. A greeting stays for a social message, not atop a substantive answer. An answer that covers no dispute is a simple, straight answer to the point asked: no In short and no dispute headings, while P39, P41's More on this and P42 apply (O13 (2)). Overrides the overview's content in P26 ('where each dispute stands, how they affect each other, what to do first'), where P27's callout stands ('the top of the overview'), and the 17-18 Oct readability design's separate In short and time-limit table.
