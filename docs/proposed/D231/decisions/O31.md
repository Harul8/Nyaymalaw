CLAUDE'S FINDINGS, 26 Oct 2026 (from source, the corpus folder's file sizes, this plan and public price lists; no cloud was rented and nothing was benchmarked):
(1) NM NEEDS A GPU SERVER. Search ranks candidates with two local models - BAAI/bge-large-en-v1.5 (queries) and BAAI/bge-reranker-v2-m3 (reranker) - on CUDA when present, else CPU (nm/core_engine/retrieval.py lines 28-29 and 218-228). The plan measures the reranker as the limit of held-law search: about 90 passages a second on the owner's RTX 4060 laptop GPU in half precision (SEQ.3), 5.8 s a legal question and 80-116 s a matter (SEQ.7, AN.4). On a CPU-only server it would be at least about 20 times slower (Claude's estimate, not measured), so a plain web server is out.
(2) WHAT IT HOLDS. The live indexes (.nm/retrieval/bare_acts.lineage.json and judgments.lineage.json): the Acts store acts_v5 (351,180 passages: vector index 1.44 GB, word index about 0.15 GB, passage store 0.72 GB) and the judgments (1,015,780 passages: vector index 4.16 GB, word index about 0.76 GB, passage store chunks.db 3.57 GB) - about 11 GB, of which the 5.6 GB of vectors are plain flat float32 indexes (each file's size is exactly vectors x 1,024 x 4 bytes plus a 45-byte header) read in full by every search, so they should stay in memory. The two models are 3.4 GB (1.25 and 2.14). The corpus folder on the laptop is 28 GB only because it also keeps older versions and backups. Sizing: a GPU with 16 GB or more (24 GB is better), at least 32 GB RAM, 250-500 GB SSD.
(3) TURN TIME AND COST (measured, Luna for every step): simple turns 67-130 s at $0.08-0.32 (S3.5, SEQ.4); two-dispute matters 322-776 s at $0.09-0.19 (AN.2-AN.6); a very large matter would cost about $1 (SEQ.4). Search is roughly a sixth to a third of a turn; the rest is model time, which no server location changes.
(4) LINUX: nothing found that blocks it - file locks have both branches (nm/arrive/store_directory.py 113-120 and 566-573), the virus check is ClamAV (nm/app/composition.py 246-259) and the OpenAI adapter's Windows branches are platform-guarded (nm/shared/model_openai_adapter.py 258 and 357). NOT YET RUN ON LINUX: the server, CUDA torch, the search warm-up and ClamAV have not been started there. Two things found on the way are recorded in AN.25: the resident virus scanner cannot start with a relative store path, and reading a scanned document is one request of about 80 s, which a proxy or load balancer with a 60-100 s limit would cut off.

CAPACITY: five users at once queue on the one GPU: a 13-question matter's search is about 50 s of GPU alone on the laptop's card (4,472 pairs at about 90 a second, AN.2), so five at the same instant would take up to about 4 minutes for the last (unmeasured on an L4; people rarely line up exactly); an L40S would cut that several-fold for about Rs 20,000 a month more at E2E (Rs 102 an hour) - not advised at this size. AUTO-STOP WHEN IDLE would cut the hours further, but the first person after a stop waits about 2-4 minutes for boot and warm-up; start with the fixed schedule and look at the real hours after a month. RECOMMENDATION: an L4-class server on a fixed 09:30-22:30 schedule, started and stopped by the provider's own scheduler (AWS, Azure and Google have one; E2E to be asked), the benchmark afternoon first (about 5 hours: Rs 250 at E2E, about Rs 560 at AWS).
PAY-PER-USE GPU, 26 Oct 2026 (the owner: "what about use per the time of GPU consumed, no dedicated GPU all the time").
