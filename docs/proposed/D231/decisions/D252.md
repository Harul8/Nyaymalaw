## From [AN.51](../rows/AN.51.md), Description

D252 (what D198 counts; 16 Nov 2026). WHAT 'NAMES A PROVISION' AND 'A SOURCE THAT IS OR NAMES IT' MEAN, mechanically. A paragraph names a provision where the reference patterns that citation_contracts owns find one by its number (section, article, rule, Order; written in full or short). It is supported where a source the paragraph uses - with the passages read beside it - carries that number as its label or standing by itself in its words. For a paragraph that sets out what the papers say (kind 'account'), the advocate's own messages and papers count, because it reports them; for every other kind only held law counts. NOT HELD TO IT, and why: a paragraph of kind 'question' or 'limitation' asks the advocate something or says what NM does not hold - it names law without stating it and by its nature has no held passage; and the overview, which by its own rule cites no held law and rests on the sections, each of which is checked. LIMITS, said: an Act named without any provision is not checked; that the provision belongs to the Act named beside it is not checked (the number is); a number that stands in a used passage for another reason passes. Whether the passage supports what is said stays the reviewer's judgment. MEASURED before building, on the answer kept in the baseline recording (3 sections, 54 paragraphs, 10 naming a provision, 20 provisions named): no paragraph is faulted by either check, so the recorded turn is unchanged by them.
