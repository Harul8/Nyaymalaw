## From [AN.51](../rows/AN.51.md), Description

D251 (how 51d and 51f are built; 16 Nov 2026). THE TWO NEW CHECKS ARE MADE WHEN A PARAGRAPH IS ADMITTED AS IT IS WRITTEN, AND NO NEW VERSION IS MADE. Found on reading the code: a new rule inside the writer's admission would, by the usual way, be a new writer version; a writer version is tied to a rendering version, and that to the turn's version, so one new rule would mean three new versions, and a matter being worked step by step across the change would hold analyses of two versions. And a rule simply added to admission would be run against every answer already saved when it is read back, and could refuse to open one (P46 forbids that). So: the checks run only where the writing collects a paragraph's faults for its second look (every admission made while a section is written or mended); an answer read back from the store is read under the rules it was saved with and is not judged again by a rule made after it. What a new answer shows is checked; nothing saved changes shape. The limit, said: on reading back, code does not check these two rules again - the saved answer is the one that passed them.
