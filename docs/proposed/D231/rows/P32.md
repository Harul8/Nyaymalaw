# P32 - Law read connected: whole provisions, the Act's own links, the sections beside

## Item

Law read connected: whole provisions, the Act's own links, the sections beside

## Description

Law read connected (step 3 of 20 Oct). The research reads a provision as a lawyer does. (1) WHOLE: every provision search returns, and so every provision the pick chooses, is read with all its parts (sub-sections, provisos, explanations, illustrations, exceptions), never only the clauses search happened to touch. (2) LINKED IN THE ACT'S OWN WORDS, one step both ways: the sections, Articles and rules a provision names, ranges such as 'sections 4 to 24' included, and the sections that name it; each link keeps the words that make it. A reference to another Act ('of the Indian Penal Code', 'of that Act', 'the said Code') is never followed into the provision's own Act; a reference the Act's held provisions cannot bind to exactly one is not followed, is kept in the record and is never shown as law. Code works the links out from the held Act the first time it is read; no corpus job. (3) NEXT TO IT: the sections immediately before and after, by heading, as a lawyer scans the contents. (4) The pick chooses from all of it in the same call: each question's search results, then the linked and neighbouring provisions by heading with why each is there; whatever it chooses is read whole. (5) The writer and the review receive each dispute's law grouped by provision ('s.138, read with s.139 ..., s.140 ...'). Not in this step: definitions limited to the terms a provision uses (measured next), the review reading in full only what the draft relies on (its own decision after this), old and new codes (the search phrases already name both), judgments (P31).

Code's bounds and rules, 20 Oct 2026 (mechanical, not legal judgement): a section over 32,000 characters (about 8,000 tokens, the line of P30 and P31; 276 of 66,684 held sections) is read in part, the parts search found with its opening, and the record says so; at most 10 links each way are shown for one returned provision (99% of held sections name at most 9 and are named by at most 6), the rest counted and said; an amending Act's references name the principal Act's provisions, and a 'section' in Rules or Regulations names the parent Act's, so neither is followed inside them; an editor's note of amendment ('Inserted by Act 21 of 2000, Section 92') is not a link.
