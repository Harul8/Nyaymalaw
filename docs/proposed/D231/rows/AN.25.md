## Description

THE OWNER, 26 Oct 2026: "when files are uploaded, how long does scanning and reading take? see if this is optimal today? for a 20 page scanned document it took more than a min for me now".
MEASURED. (a) The owner's own upload this evening (.nm/egress.log, which labels nothing, so the start is inferred): the request's first log lines are at 18:31:52 IST (the same second the scanner's configuration file was last rewritten); the original was sealed at 18:33:19; the AI began reading at 18:33:25 and made 20 calls, one a page, four at a time, the last starting at 18:34:27 and ending about 18:34:42. So: before sealing (malware scan and local reading) up to about 87 s; from sealing to the first page read 6 s (unsealing, drawing the pages); AI reading about 77 s (about 15 s a call, 8-20 s). About 2 min 50 s from the click, not 'more than a minute'. (b) Claude's timing of the local steps on four synthetic 20-page scanned PDFs of 8.4 MB (no private data, no model call; the machine busy with the running server): the command-line scanner needs 31.3 s to load its definitions even for a tiny file (11 s on 15 Oct, SEQ.4); the resident engine takes 28.5 s to load once, then 38-39 s to scan one such PDF (0.13 s for a file it has seen before); receive (scan + local reading + sealing) 37.9 s, so the scan is nearly all of it; drawing the 20 pages for the AI 1.8 s (JPEG 394-397 KB each). With the engine's image fuzzy-hash option off, the same kind of PDF scanned in 3.3 s: the other 35 s was ClamAV hashing the page images.
THE DEFECT (found by this check): the resident scanner has never started on this installation. ClamAVCheck writes the engine's configuration with the store folder as given - NM_MATTER_STORE is the relative '.nm/matters' - so LogFile and TemporaryDirectory are relative, and clamd refuses to start: running it with the live configuration prints 'ERROR: LogFile requires full path.' No clamd.log is written and no clamd process remains; every check re-attempts the start, fails, and falls back to the command-line scanner, which reloads 3.6 million signatures each time and runs one at a time (several files queue). The tests use absolute temporary folders, which is why they pass. SEQ.6's note that the virus check 'kept running from the start of the app' was wrong for this configuration; corrected there.
THE OTHER COSTS: (1) pages are read 4 at a time (nm/core_engine/page_reading.py line 25; no owner decision records the 4), so 20 pages are 5 waves of about 15 s; (2) reading is one request of 80+ s that the browser holds, with only a 'reading scans' chip and no page count (nm/app/app.js 4924-4942, nm/app/api.py 1781-1809); behind a proxy or load balancer that cuts requests at 60 s (the AWS load balancer's default) or 100 s (Cloudflare's free proxy) a scanned document of about 25 pages or more would fail - this matters for hosting (O31); (3) the AI's per-page instructions and its 'low' reasoning setting are the owner's (13 Oct reasoning-loop rule) and are not changed.
PIECES (one production file each, with focused tests): P1, A DEFECT, built 26 Oct 2026 - every path handed to a ClamAV program made absolute once (nm/open_matter/clamav_check.py), so the resident engine starts; P2, DECIDED BY THE OWNER 26 Oct 2026 ("turn it off"), built - the image fuzzy-hash option off, passed only where the scanner's own help lists it (an older ClamAV has no such option and refuses one it does not know, which would hold every document), and checked against the real ClamAV with the EICAR test string, which must still be held in the resident engine and in the command-line fallback (same file): about 38 s to 3 s for this 20-page scan; it only stops ClamAV matching page images against its fingerprints of known-bad images (a phishing-lookalike defence), not its other checks of a PDF; P3, owner's go-ahead and a bounded live run (about 20 page reads, under $0.10) - more pages read at once with a limit shared by all uploads (nm/core_engine/page_reading.py), the number chosen from the run; P4, owner's go-ahead - reading as a background job with page-by-page progress (nm/app/api.py, nm/app/app.js). ESTIMATES, not measured: P1 saves about 31 s an upload; P1 + P2 take the scan of this 20-page file from about 70 s to about 3-4 s; P3 at 10-20 at once takes the reading from 77 s to about 20-35 s; together a 20-page scan from about 2 min 50 s to about 30-45 s. NOT CHANGED: that no file is read before the malware check clears it. THE READING STARTS AT ONCE: the request that starts it answered in 0.02 s (202, state 'reading'), after an upload of 0.6 s. NEVER MORE THAN 8 AT ONCE (P3): the most pages in flight was 8, and one page took 7.1 to 11.8 s (median 9.6 s). PROGRESS: 1 page read by 10.2 s, 8 by 13.6 s, 16 by 21.8 s, all 20 by 30.5 s - three waves, as P3 planned, but of about 10 s, not the 15 s the plan assumed (so about 30 s, not 45). The job ended 'done', 20 of 20 pages read by the AI, and the page that asked after it every second would have asked about 30 times. COST: 20 calls, $0.016 (66,240 tokens in, 18,332 out), $0.0008 a page; the estimate was under $0.10. NOT MEASURED: the same upload before P3 (by arithmetic, five waves of four at about 10 s, about 50 s); the malware check's own time; reading QUALITY - 19 of the 20 pages came back 'partly legible', which says these drawn pages are harder than a real scan or the page image is smaller than the type wants, so this run speaks for time and parallelism only. AN OPTION FOR THE OWNER (not changed): reading 10 pages at once would make 20 pages two waves, about 20 s; the shared limit of 16 would still hold. FOUND ON THE WAY (not built): the harness first failed with 'document_storage' (HTTP 503, 'could not be kept safely') because the sealed custody names are long and a store under a deep folder passes Windows' 260 characters; the page is told only that the document could not be kept, not why. An installation under a short folder is not affected; one under a deep folder would be, and the operator would have to find it in the log.
