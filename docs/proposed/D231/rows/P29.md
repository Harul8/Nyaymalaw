The independent review withholds only what misleads

## Description

The independent review withholds only what misleads. (1) It answers two plain questions about a section: which statements would mislead the advocate as they stand - not supported by their messages, the documents they sent or the held law; a quotation that is not the source's words; the wrong speaker or court; a claim that NM did something - and what the section could add or put better. A statement that still misleads after the third try is left out (P25); what could be better, including what could go (P43), drives one rewrite (P33), and what is still suggested after it is kept under a folded 'How NM checked this' at the end of the answer (P39, 24 Oct 2026). It lists no requests and gives no verdict: code decides from what it finds. (2) Its own slips never cost a section: code keeps what it can use and sets the rest aside; only a review with nothing usable is tried again, on its own allowance, never the section's rewrites. (3) A finding that names no paragraph never removes a section: where it says something may mislead, it is said beside the section; where it says only what could be better, it goes with the other suggestions under 'How NM checked this' (P39, 24 Oct 2026). A finding about a worked-out time limit is said beside that limit. (4) On time limits it checks only that the answer contradicts none, gives no date of its own and presents no limit that was not assessed as worked out (code shows each worked-out limit in full). (5) Every review output code refused, and every draft that never got through, is kept in the matter's encrypted record, so that any withholding can be traced. A smaller review input (the law the draft relies on in full, the rest by heading) waits for connected reading (step 3).
