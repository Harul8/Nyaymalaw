## Description

THE OWNER'S WORDS, 2 Nov 2026 (after AN.35's paid runs found a dispute's section now costs about $0.50, against about $0.09): "reading 35 judgments is way too much ... At the same time, we should be able to look at all the relevant judgments ... Let the model read only the retrieved passages, and then let the model decide which of those passages are relevant, and let the model decide those top 2, 3, 4, or 5 judgments which are most relevant, and then the model can read the full judgment of only those". Asked four questions, they answered: "1- yes 2- keep 20 for now 3 - yes 4 - now test in small scale one or two docs , ok for ankther 1$".
D34 (agreed): (1) each dispute reads in full at most five judgments, the model choosing which from the passages it chose; the judgments the papers cite that it chooses count within those five; every other judgment it chose is read by the paragraphs it chose, each with the paragraph either side. (2) Each question is still shown twenty judgment paragraphs. (3) Each label around the held passages is said once, and the second looks and the take-ups are shown only the law the section relies on, not the whole dispute's. (4) Tried in small runs on one or two papers within a new $1; applied only on the owner's word.
SPEC: Sees - the answer cites the passages chosen and the few judgments read in full, and says which were read in full. Must never - present a judgment as read in full when only passages were read, or drop a judgment the model chose. When it fails - where the choice of judgments to read in full cannot be made, the dispute keeps the passages chosen, and that is said. Passing - on the owner's papers each section's input is far below AN.35's (about 365 thousand tokens), every chosen judgment still stands by its passages, and the owner judges the answer no worse.
THE OWNER'S ANSWERS, 3 Nov 2026, to the two findings: "1- cited judgments always get first claim 2- yes".
D35 (1) The held judgments the papers cite that a dispute's further call chooses have first claim on its five places read in full; the first pick's named judgments come next, then the further pick's. (2) Agreed as put: "show those neighbours by heading only unless the model picks them". CORRECTION before any code: measured on run A's saved record, the section passages are the 90 sections the picks chose (108 thousand characters) and the other parts of those same sections - their sub-sections, provisos and explanations (393 passages, 127 thousand characters); no neighbouring section is shown unless a pick chose it. So (2) cannot be built as put, and it is not built; the owner is asked again (where the chosen sections come from: in run A the first picks chose 71 - 35 found by search, 26 named by number, 10 linked by their Acts - and the further picks 42, 20 of them linked).
THE OWNER, 3 Nov 2026, on the sections: "generally for a given dispute and a paricular part of that dispute there won't be more than lets say 2-5 relevants section from same act or different acts -- so, reading a lot more of passages means we are reading unrelated passages mosyly"; shown (offline, AN.36's three runs) that each question chose 9-28 sections while the answer cited about two a question, and that a cut at the first five of each question's list would have lost 8 of the 40 sections the answer cited and 15 of the 49 the case box quoted, they said "go ahead" to the proposal.
D36: the provision pick chooses, for each question, the two to five provisions that govern it - the rule and what qualifies or completes it (its exceptions, conditions, time limit, procedure), most necessary first; code holds each question to five; sections named by number and sections linked by their Acts count within the five; the further search fills only what is left of each question's five. Tried on runs A and B again within the $0.37 left; applied only on the owner's word. If the case box's supporting law thins out, the limit rises to seven or definitions and the proof of documents stand outside the five.
